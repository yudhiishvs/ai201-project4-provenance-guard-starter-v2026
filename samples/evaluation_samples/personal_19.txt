I have become more cautious about treating productivity tools as solutions to organizational problems. It is tempting to download a new application whenever tasks start piling up. For a few days, the novelty can make everything feel under control. But if the underlying problem is unclear priorities, the tool simply provides a more attractive place to store an overwhelming list. I have found that deciding what not to do is often more useful than creating another dashboard. At the start of a busy week, I try to identify the few commitments that truly have deadlines and the ones that can wait. I still use calendars and task lists, but I want them to support decisions rather than replace the process of making them.
