I think the design of everyday software matters most when users are tired, distracted, or unfamiliar with the system. An interface may look elegant during a demonstration but become frustrating when someone needs to correct a mistake quickly. I noticed this while helping a friend complete an online form. The page had several required fields, yet it only explained the missing information after submission. Worse, the error message did not identify which field needed attention. We eventually completed the form, but the process felt unnecessarily difficult. Since then, I have paid more attention to error messages and recovery paths when evaluating software. A good interface should not require users to understand its internal rules before they can recover from an ordinary mistake.
