I have noticed that I understand a programming concept better when I can explain why a simpler approach fails. For example, it is easy to memorize that a hash table can speed up repeated lookups. It is harder to recognize when maintaining that table adds unnecessary complexity. While practicing problems, I sometimes begin with a straightforward solution even if I suspect it will be too slow. Writing it down helps me identify which operation is repeated and what information could be stored. Then I can make a specific improvement rather than guessing which data structure the problem expects. This method takes more time at the beginning, but it has made my solutions easier to reason about. I also find that I remember the underlying idea longer.
