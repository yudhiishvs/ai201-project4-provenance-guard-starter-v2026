I used to think that a good study session meant spending as many hours as possible at my desk. That approach worked occasionally, but it also produced long stretches where I was technically studying without learning much. Recently, I have started dividing difficult assignments into smaller tasks. Before opening my laptop, I write down the exact question I want to answer. Sometimes that means understanding a single theorem; other times it means debugging one function. This approach makes it easier to notice when I am stuck. It also gives me a clear stopping point. I still have days when I procrastinate, especially when the assignment looks intimidating. The difference is that I now have a practical way to begin instead of waiting until I feel motivated.
