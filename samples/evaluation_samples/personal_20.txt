A good experiment should make it possible to learn something even when the result is disappointing. I used to think of experiments mainly as attempts to prove that an idea works. That mindset encourages small changes to the setup until the numbers look better, which can make the conclusion less trustworthy. Now I see more value in deciding what counts as success before looking at the results. It also helps to keep the comparison fair by changing one variable at a time when possible. If the outcome is worse, I want to know whether the proposed change caused the decline or whether some other condition shifted. A negative result with a clear explanation can guide the next attempt more effectively than an impressive number whose origin is uncertain.
