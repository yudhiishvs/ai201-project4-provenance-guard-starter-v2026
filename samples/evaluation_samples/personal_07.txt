One thing I find interesting about public transportation is how much a small delay can affect an entire trip. A bus arriving five minutes late may seem insignificant, but that delay can cause someone to miss a train that only runs every half hour. The traveler then experiences a much larger delay than the original five minutes. I noticed this while planning a trip that involved two transfers. The schedule looked efficient on paper, but there was almost no margin for error. I ended up choosing a slightly longer route with fewer connections. It was less elegant, but it felt more reliable. That experience reminded me that optimizing for the best possible outcome is different from planning for the outcome most likely to occur.
