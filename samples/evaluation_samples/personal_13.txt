A difficult part of learning statistics is accepting that a convincing pattern may still be misleading. If two variables move together, it is tempting to tell a story about one causing the other. Yet there may be a third factor influencing both, or the pattern may depend on how the data was collected. I have started paying more attention to the questions that come before a calculation. Who was included in the sample? What observations are missing? Was the measurement consistent? These questions do not always produce a neat numerical answer, but they shape whether the result means anything. I like that statistics combines mathematical tools with skepticism. It asks me not only to calculate an estimate, but also to examine how much confidence that estimate deserves.
