The first time I looked closely at a financial statement, I was surprised by how different profit and cash could be. I had assumed that a profitable business would naturally have plenty of money available. Then I learned how unpaid customer invoices, inventory purchases, and equipment spending can change the picture. A company might report strong earnings while still facing pressure to pay its bills. That distinction made financial analysis feel more concrete. Numbers are not just answers to formulas; they describe events that happened at different times. I now try to ask what each number represents before comparing it with another. Even a basic question about timing can reveal why two measures that sound similar tell different stories.
