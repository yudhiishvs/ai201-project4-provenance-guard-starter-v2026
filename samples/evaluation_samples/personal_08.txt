During a group project, I learned that dividing work equally is not always the same as dividing it effectively. Our first plan assigned everyone a similar number of tasks. Unfortunately, some tasks depended on others being finished first, so several people spent time waiting. We eventually reorganized the project around dependencies rather than task counts. One person handled the data format, another built the interface, and I worked on connecting the two. We also started writing down assumptions instead of relying on verbal agreements. The final result was not perfect, but collaboration became much smoother. The biggest lesson for me was that coordination is part of the technical work. A team can have capable members and still struggle if nobody knows how their pieces fit together.
